## LinkedIn

Electrical faults are one of the most common causes of fire in commercial premises. In a typical office, workshop, or retail unit, the everyday risks include extension leads running under carpets, sockets loaded with four-way adapters, and appliances that have never had a formal check.

PAT testing (portable appliance testing) is not a legal requirement in itself, but it is a recognised way to demonstrate that electrical equipment is safe and that you have documented the check. It is also, in practice, the kind of evidence that matters after an incident.

A few things worth looking at now:

- Extension leads and multi-socket adapters: are they appropriate for the load being put through them?
- Any appliances brought in from outside (kettles, fans, portable heaters, phone chargers) that have never been checked?
- A record of when each appliance was last tested, and when it is due?

Electrical safety should be part of your fire risk assessment, not a separate afterthought. It is one of the areas we cover when we carry out an assessment. If your last one did not address it clearly, that is worth revisiting.
