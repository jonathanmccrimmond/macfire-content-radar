## Angle

## LinkedIn

If a fire safety inspector or your insurer asks to see your fire log book, the entries in it tell a clear story: either fire safety is being managed actively and regularly, or it is not.

A fire log book should record:

- Weekly fire alarm test results, including the zone or call point tested, and any faults.
- Fire drills: date, number of people, time to evacuate, any issues noted.
- Any alarm activations: genuine, false, or test, and the cause if known.
- Maintenance and servicing visits for alarms, extinguishers, emergency lighting, and fire doors.
- Any fire safety training sessions, and who attended.

The format does not need to be complicated, a bound book or a simple template, but it needs to be filled in consistently. Gaps in the record, or a book that has not been updated for months, are as telling as the entries themselves.

Keeping that record, prompting the weekly test, and making sure servicing visits are logged, is part of what we include in a managed fire safety arrangement. If your current log book does not reflect what is actually happening on site, that is worth fixing before an inspection or incident makes it matter.
