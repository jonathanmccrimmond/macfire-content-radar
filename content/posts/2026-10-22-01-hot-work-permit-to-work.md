## Angle

Hot work by contractors is one of the most common causes of premises fires. A permit-to-work system manages the risk before, during, and after the work. MacFire advises on compliant procedures.

## LinkedIn

Hot work, cutting, welding, grinding, and anything else that generates heat or sparks, is one of the most common causes of accidental fires in commercial and industrial premises. Most of those fires happen after the contractor has left, when smouldering materials go undetected.

A permit-to-work system is the practical fix. Before work starts, it confirms:

- The area has been assessed and flammable materials cleared or protected.
- A fire watch is in place during work and for at least an hour after.
- Extinguishers and detection are available and checked.
- Isolation of alarm zones is managed correctly (not just disabled and forgotten).

Without that structure, the responsibility often falls through the gap between the responsible person and the contractor. If hot work is a regular part of your site's activity, or if contractors come in seasonally, getting the procedure right is worth doing properly. It is something we can advise on.
