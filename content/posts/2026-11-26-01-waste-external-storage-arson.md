## Angle

External waste, cardboard, and storage left against building walls is one of the most common arson vectors and an easily preventable fire risk. A practical reminder for businesses and hospitality.

## LinkedIn

Arson accounts for a significant share of commercial building fires, and the majority of those fires start with whatever is easiest to ignite at the side or back of a building.

A quick look at what sits against your external walls:

- Cardboard and packaging waiting for the next collection, stacked directly against the wall.
- A skip or large bin that is left open and accessible overnight.
- Old furniture, pallets, or stock lined up by the service entrance.
- A delivery area that doubles as informal storage.

None of this takes a determined arsonist, just an opportunistic one with a lighter. The straightforward fix is clear separation: combustible waste away from the building envelope, bins and skips covered or locked overnight, and a collection schedule that stops material building up.

A fire risk assessment should flag this, but it is also the kind of thing that slips between maintenance cycles. Worth walking the outside of your building with that question in mind.
