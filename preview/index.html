The fire log book: what to record and why it matters

2026-12-10 No source listed Source date unknown
Draft
If it is not recorded, it did not happen

Your fire log book is the evidence that fire safety is being managed properly. It should record weekly alarm tests, drills, activations, servicing visits, and training.

Gaps in the record are as bad as missing checks. Keeping it up to date, and prompting the weekly test and annual service, is part of what we include in a managed fire safety arrangement.

#FireSafety #FireLogBook

LinkedIn · MacFire Ltd Fire Protection page
MacFire Ltd Fire Protection
Fire Protection Services at MacFire Ltd
2 h ·

If a fire safety inspector or your insurer asks to see your fire log book, the entries in it tell a clear story: either fire safety is being managed actively and regularly, or it is not.

A fire log book should record:

- Weekly fire alarm test results, including the zone or call point tested, and any faults.
- Fire drills: date, number of people, time to evacuate, any issues noted.
- Any alarm activations: genuine, false, or test, and the cause if known.
- Maintenance and servicing visits for alarms, extinguishers, emergency lighting, and fire doors.
- Any fire safety training sessions, and who attended.

The format does not need to be complicated, a bound book or a simple template, but it needs to be filled in consistently. Gaps in the record, or a book that has not been updated for months, are as telling as the entries themselves.

Keeping that record, prompting the weekly test, and making sure servicing visits are logged, is part of what we include in a managed fire safety arrangement. If your current log book does not reflect what is actually happening on site, that is worth fixing before an inspection or incident makes it matter.

Waste and storage around your building: the arson risk

2026-11-26 No source listed Source date unknown
Draft
Facebook · MacFire Ltd page
MacFire Ltd

One of the most common arson risks to commercial buildings is what is left against the outside: cardboard waiting for collection, an open skip, pallets stacked by the back door.

The fix is straightforward: combustibles away from the wall, bins covered overnight, regular waste clearance.

Worth a walk around the outside of your premises with that thought in mind.

#FireSafety #ArsonPrevention

LinkedIn · MacFire Ltd Fire Protection page
MacFire Ltd Fire Protection
Fire Protection Services at MacFire Ltd
2 h ·

Arson accounts for a significant share of commercial building fires, and the majority of those fires start with whatever is easiest to ignite at the side or back of a building.

A quick look at what sits against your external walls:

- Cardboard and packaging waiting for the next collection, stacked directly against the wall.
- A skip or large bin that is left open and accessible overnight.
- Old furniture, pallets, or stock lined up by the service entrance.
- A delivery area that doubles as informal storage.

None of this takes a determined arsonist, just an opportunistic one with a lighter. The straightforward fix is clear separation: combustible waste away from the building envelope, bins and skips covered or locked overnight, and a collection schedule that stops material building up.

A fire risk assessment should flag this, but it is also the kind of thing that slips between maintenance cycles. Worth walking the outside of your building with that question in mind.

Landlord fire safety: what you are responsible for

2026-11-19 No source listed Source date unknown
Draft
The responsible person for a rented property is you
0141 881 5455
Facebook · MacFire Ltd page
MacFire Ltd
2 h ·

As a landlord, fire safety is your responsibility. The basics: smoke alarms on every floor (interlinked), a heat detector in the kitchen, fire doors in working order, and clear escape routes.

For an HMO with three or more tenants, a formal fire risk assessment is a legal requirement. Carrying those out is something we do.

#FireSafety #LandlordSafety #HMO

LinkedIn · MacFire Ltd Fire Protection page
MacFire Ltd Fire Protection
Fire Protection Services at MacFire Ltd
2 h ·

If you are a landlord, you are the responsible person under fire safety law for your rented property, which means the obligation to manage fire risk sits with you, not the tenant.

The basics that should be in place for most rented properties:

- Smoke alarms on every floor, heat detector in the kitchen, all interlinked.
- A carbon monoxide alarm where there is a solid fuel appliance.
- Fire doors in the right places, in working condition, with self-closers.
- Clear escape routes: no blocked corridors, no stored items in stairwells.
- Emergency lighting for any shared internal area where the layout means a tenant could be disoriented in a power cut.

For an HMO (a property rented to three or more people from different households) a formal written fire risk assessment is a legal requirement, not optional. That assessment should be carried out by a competent person and reviewed when anything significant changes.

If you are managing one or several properties and are not certain what is in place, a straightforward review is the starting point. Carrying out landlord and HMO fire risk assessments is something we do.

Cigarette waste causes real fires. The common issues: a bin right next to a door or gas meter, a plastic bin instead of a proper metal stub-out, or a shelter that has become a cardboard store.

The fix is straightforward. A compliant setup takes minutes to agree and is part of any good premises fire risk assessment. We can sort it.

LinkedIn · MacFire Ltd Fire Protection page

Cigarette waste is one of the more preventable causes of external building fires, and yet the setup of smoking areas at many commercial premises does not get much thought beyond putting a bin near the door.

A few things that go wrong in practice:

- A metal stub-out bin placed directly against a timber cladding panel, or next to a gas meter.
- A plastic bin used instead of a proper metal-hooded stub-out bin, because it was what was available.
- A smoking shelter that has become an informal storage area for cardboard and packaging.
- Bins that are emptied infrequently, so waste builds up before it is fully out.

The standard is not complicated: a suitable metal bin, at a safe distance from combustible materials and building openings, cleared regularly, in a position that does not encourage smoking near fire exits or deliveries. If your current setup has drifted from that, it is a quick thing to put right. Getting it properly reviewed is something we can do as part of a wider premises assessment.

Electrical safety and fire: the everyday risk

2026-11-05 No source listed Source date unknown

Electrical faults cause a large share of workplace fires. The common culprits are overloaded sockets, extension leads under carpets, and appliances that have never been checked.

PAT testing documents that your equipment is safe. Your fire risk assessment should be covering this too. If yours did not, it might be time for a proper review. That is something we carry out.

#FireSafety #PATTesting #ElectricalSafety

LinkedIn · MacFire Ltd Fire Protection page
MacFire Ltd Fire Protection
Fire Protection Services at MacFire Ltd
2 h ·

Electrical faults are one of the most common causes of fire in commercial premises. In a typical office, workshop, or retail unit, the everyday risks include extension leads running under carpets, sockets loaded with four-way adapters, and appliances that have never had a formal check.

PAT testing (portable appliance testing) is not a legal requirement in itself, but it is a recognised way to demonstrate that electrical equipment is safe and that you have documented the check. It is also, in practice, the kind of evidence that matters after an incident.

A few things worth looking at now:

- Extension leads and multi-socket adapters: are they appropriate for the load being put through them?
- Any appliances brought in from outside (kettles, fans, portable heaters, phone chargers) that have never been checked?
- A record of when each appliance was last tested, and when it is due?

Electrical safety should be part of your fire risk assessment, not a separate afterthought. It is one of the areas we cover when we carry out an assessment. If your last one did not address it clearly, that is worth revisiting.

Hot work on site: the permit-to-work habit

2026-10-22 No source listed Source date unknown
Draft
Every contractor on site needs a permit
0141 881 5455
Facebook · MacFire Ltd page
MacFire Ltd
2 h ·

Hot work is one of the most common causes of premises fires, often after the contractor has left.

A permit-to-work system sorts the risk: area check before work starts, fire watch during and for an hour after, extinguishers in place, alarm zones managed properly.

Getting that procedure right is something we can advise on.

#FireSafety #HotWork

LinkedIn · MacFire Ltd Fire Protection page
MacFire Ltd Fire Protection
Fire Protection Services at MacFire Ltd
2 h ·

Hot work, cutting, welding, grinding, and anything else that generates heat or sparks, is one of the most common causes of accidental fires in commercial and industrial premises. Most of those fires happen after the contractor has left, when smouldering materials go undetected.

A permit-to-work system is the practical fix. Before work starts, it confirms:

- The area has been assessed and flammable materials cleared or protected.
- A fire watch is in place during work and for at least an hour after.
- Extinguishers and detection are available and checked.
- Isolation of alarm zones is managed correctly (not just disabled and forgotten).

Without that structure, the responsibility often falls through the gap between the responsible person and the contractor. If hot work is a regular part of your site's activity, or if contractors come in seasonally, getting the procedure right is worth doing properly. It is something we can advise on.
